WHY IT MATTERS

When big money moves, the exchange tells you.

01
Big investors trade in size. Mutual funds, foreign investors, insurers and company founders.
02
Big trades must be reported. NSE and BSE publish them every day, with the investor's name, the quantity and the price.
03
So anyone can see them. It's one of the few places you can see who is buying and who is selling.

Source: SEBI and stock exchange disclosure rules.

BULK DEAL

More than 0.5% of a company, in one day.

0.5%
When one investor buys or sells more than 0.5% of a company's listed shares in a single trading day.
·
Happens in normal market hours, in one trade or many.
·
The broker reports it to the exchange.
·
The exchange publishes it the same day.

Source: SEBI; NSE bulk deal disclosures.

BLOCK DEAL

₹25 crore or more, in one trade.

₹25 Cr+
One large trade, agreed between a buyer and a seller, in a special window.
·
Two 15-minute windows: 8:45–9:00 AM and 2:05–2:20 PM.
·
Price limit: within ±3% of a reference price.
·
Real delivery: the shares must change hands. No squaring off the same day.

Source: SEBI's revised block deal framework (circular of 8 Oct 2025).

HOW TO READ IT

What a big trade tells you, and what it doesn't.

IT TELLS YOU

Who traded

How much, and at what price

Whether funds or foreign investors are involved

IT DOESN'T TELL YOU

Why they traded

Where the price goes next

A seller may just need cash. A buyer may be rebalancing.

One trade on its own
is not a trend.
