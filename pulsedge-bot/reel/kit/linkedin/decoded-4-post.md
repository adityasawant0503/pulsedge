## LinkedIn post text

Silver is about 64% below January's record.
It has also been short of metal for six straight years.

Both are true, and that gap is this week's Decoded.

On 27 Nov, UBS turned positive on silver. Its reasons were the gold/silver ratio at 88 and the Fed finishing its hikes by early 2027. The shortage wasn't one of them: UBS sees the market closer to balance.

Three things from the note:

1. The deficit is small. This year's 35 Moz is about 3% of demand, next to roughly 1.25 bn oz held in ETPs. Six years of shortfall were covered from vaults.

2. Since September, silver has traded on the Fed. In October it fell 18% while gold fell about 6–7%.

3. It has crashed like this before, in 1980, 2008 and 2011. How each crash ended depended on the Fed far more than on silver.

For Indian buyers there's a fourth dial: import duty, raised from 6% to 15% in May. A cut in the 1 Feb Budget would lower the rupee price even if global silver rose.

The signposts I'm watching are on the last slide. Which one would you add?
